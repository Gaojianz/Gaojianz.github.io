Living Earth Unit 3 Review — Ecosystems: Matter & Energy

Part 1 · Photosynthesis, Cellular Respiration & Fermentation

1. ATP — the cell’s energy “battery”

  • ATP = adenosine + 3 phosphates; energy is released when the last phosphate bond breaks → ADP + Pi.
  • Recycled: respiration (and the light reactions) re-attach P to ADP → ATP. Cells reuse the same ADP/ATP over and over.
  • Powers active transport, muscle contraction, building molecules (anabolism).
From: Lecture: ATP (10/9); CK-12 2.17–2.24
Vocab: ATP, ADP, phosphate
  1. What happens to ATP when a cell uses its energy? → It loses a phosphate → ADP + Pi + energy
  2. Where does the energy to rebuild ATP come from in animals? → Breaking down glucose in cellular respiration

2. The equations (memorize!)

  • Photosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light → C6H12O6 + 6O2 (anabolic: builds sugar, stores energy).
  • Aerobic respiration: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ~36–38 ATP (catabolic).
  • Lactic acid fermentation: pyruvate + NADH → lactic acid + NAD+ (muscles, yogurt bacteria).
  • Alcoholic fermentation: pyruvate + NADH → ethanol + CO2 + NAD+ (yeast).
From: Photo Summary Q #1; Resp Summary Q #1, #13
Vocab: reactant, product, anabolic, catabolic, aerobic, anaerobic
  1. Label reactants of photosynthesis. → CO2, H2O (+ light energy)
  2. Why is photosynthesis anabolic? → It builds a large molecule (glucose) from small ones and stores energy
  3. Which fermentation makes bread rise? → Alcoholic — the CO2 gas

3. How photosynthesis & respiration depend on each other

  • Products of one = reactants of the other: PS makes glucose + O2; respiration uses them and returns CO2 + H2O.
  • Plants do BOTH (respire day and night); animals only respire.
  • Lab idea: bromothymol blue (BTB) turns yellow with CO2 (acid), back to blue/green when CO2 is removed. Elodea in light → BTB blue (PS > respiration); Elodea in dark → yellow (respiration only). Snail alone → yellow.
  • Controls: tube with no organism; same light/temp/volume. Independent variable = light (or organism); dependent = color/CO2.
From: Photosynthesis & Cellular Respiration Lab (10/20–21) + lab handout; PS and CR graphic organizer (10/19)
Vocab: bromothymol blue, control, independent/dependent variable
  1. Elodea + snail in BTB kept in the dark turns yellow. Why? → Both only respire in the dark → CO2 builds up
  2. Why include a tube of BTB with no organism? → Control — shows color change is caused by the organisms
  3. Elodea + snail in light stays blue. Explain. → Plant’s photosynthesis uses up the CO2 both release

4. Inside photosynthesis (chloroplast)

  • Light-dependent reactions — thylakoid membrane: light hits chlorophyll in photosystem II; water is split → e−, H+, and O2 released (leaves via stomata).
  • e− move down the ETC (proteins) → energy pumps H+ into thylakoid space → chemiosmosis: H+ flows through ATP synthase → ATP.
  • PSI re-energizes e− → final acceptor NADP+ → NADPH (in stroma).
  • Calvin cycle — stroma: CO2 fixed by rubisco; ATP + NADPH used → G3P → glucose; RuBP regenerated. Extra sugar stored as starch.
From: BioInteractive Photosynthesis animation WS; Photo Summary Qs (due 10/19); Role of C-cycling in Photo WS
Vocab: thylakoid, stroma, photosystem, chemiosmosis, NADPH, rubisco, G3P, Calvin cycle
  1. Where does the O2 released by plants come from? → Splitting water (H2O) in photosystem II
  2. Which 2 products of the light reactions go to the Calvin cycle? → ATP and NADPH
  3. Where does most of a tree’s mass come from? → CO2 from the air (fixed in the Calvin cycle)

5. Inside cellular respiration (cytoplasm → mitochondrion)

  • Glycolysis — cytoplasm: glucose → 2 pyruvate; uses 2 ATP, makes 4 → net 2 ATP + 2 NADH. No O2 needed.
  • Krebs cycle — matrix: releases CO2; makes NADH, FADH2, 2 ATP.
  • ETC — inner membrane: NADH/FADH2 drop e−; H+ pumped into intermembrane space; flow back through ATP synthase → ~32–34 ATP.
  • O2 is the final e− acceptor → forms H2O. Total ≈ 2 + 2 + 32–34 = 36–38 ATP.
From: Intro to Cell Resp (10/15); Aerobic Resp notes (10/16); Resp Summary Qs (due 10/22); CK-12 2.25–2.30
Vocab: glycolysis, pyruvate, Krebs cycle, matrix, NADH, FADH2, ETC, ATP synthase
  1. Where are H+ ions most concentrated in a mitochondrion? → Intermembrane space
  2. Which stage makes the most ATP? → Electron transport chain (~32–34)
  3. Where does the CO2 you exhale come from? → Krebs cycle (and pyruvate breakdown)

6. Fermentation & other anaerobic respiration

  • No O2 → ETC stops → NADH piles up. Fermentation turns NADH back into NAD+ so glycolysis can keep making 2 ATP. That’s why it’s needed even though it makes no ATP itself.
  • Happens in the cytoplasm. Products (lactic acid, ethanol) are toxic in large amounts.
  • Human uses: lactic → yogurt, cheese, sauerkraut, kimchi; alcoholic → bread, beer, wine. Muscles make lactic acid in hard exercise.
  • Other anaerobic respiration: some bacteria use nitrate or sulfate instead of O2 (e.g. denitrifying bacteria return N2 to the air) → links to the nitrogen cycle.
  • Fatigued Fingers Lab: squeezes/minute drop with repeated trials → muscles shift to lactic acid fermentation; rest (O2 returns) restores rate; raising the hand reduces blood/O2 → faster fatigue. % change = (final − initial)/initial × 100.
From: Anaerobic Respiration (10/19); Fatigued Fingers Lab (10/22) + CK-12 6.2–6.4 Reading Qs
Vocab: fermentation, lactic acid, ethanol, NAD+, anaerobic, oxygen debt
  1. Fermentation makes 0 ATP. Why do cells do it? → Regenerates NAD+ so glycolysis keeps making 2 ATP
  2. Trial 1: 60 squeezes, trial 5: 42. % change? → (42 − 60)/60 × 100 = −30%
  3. What is the final e− acceptor in lactic acid fermentation? → Pyruvate (becomes lactic acid)

7. Pigments, plastids & “what if it breaks?”

  • Chlorophyll a/b absorb red & blue, reflect green; carotenoids (yellow/orange) absorb other colors and protect. More pigments = more usable light.
  • Plastids: chloroplast (chlorophyll), chromoplast (colored pigments in fruit/flowers), leucoplast (no pigment; stores starch/oils in roots, seeds).
  • Interruptions: no light → no ATP/NADPH → Calvin cycle stops → no sugar. ETC blocked (e.g. cyanide) or no O2 → no ATP from ETC → only glycolysis + fermentation.
  • Final e− acceptors: photosynthesis = NADP+; respiration = O2; fermentation = pyruvate/acetaldehyde.
From: Lecture: Plastids & Mitochondrion; Absorption of Chlorophyll WS (10/15)
Vocab: chlorophyll, carotenoid, plastid, leucoplast, absorption spectrum
  1. Why do leaves look green? → Chlorophyll reflects green light (absorbs red & blue)
  2. A plant is kept in total dark. Which stage stops first? → Light-dependent reactions → then Calvin cycle runs out of ATP/NADPH
  3. Why are leucoplasts important? → They store food (starch) in non-green parts like roots and seeds

Part 2 · Energy Flow & Biogeochemical Cycles

8. Earth as a system of systems

  • Spheres: atmosphere (air), hydrosphere (water), biosphere (life), geosphere (rock/soil), anthrosphere (human-made).
  • They interact: plants (bio) take CO2 (atmo) and water (hydro) from soil (geo); burning fossil fuels (anthro) adds CO2 to atmo.
From: Lecture: Energy Flow & Nutrient Cycles (10/26)
Vocab: biotic, abiotic, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere, geosphere
  1. Name one biosphere–atmosphere interaction. → Photosynthesis removes CO2; respiration adds it
  2. Is soil biotic or abiotic? → Abiotic (minerals, water) — though it contains biotic organisms

9. Food chains, webs & trophic levels

  • Autotrophs/producers make food (photosynthesis or chemosynthesis at deep-sea vents). Heterotrophs/consumers eat others; decomposers recycle.
  • Arrows point in the direction energy flows: grass → rabbit → fox ("is eaten by").
  • Predator–prey: prey numbers rise first, predators follow; predators are always fewer because less energy reaches them.
From: Energy and Ecosystems WS (due Thu 10/29); Data Nuggets (11/2)
Vocab: autotroph, heterotroph, producer, consumer, chemosynthesis, food chain, food web, trophic level
  1. In grass → grasshopper → frog → snake, what trophic level is the frog? → 3rd (secondary consumer)
  2. If frogs disappear, what happens to grasshoppers? → Increase at first (fewer predators), then may crash after overeating grass

10. Ecological pyramids & the 10% rule

  • Only ~10% of energy passes to the next level; ~90% is lost as heat (respiration) and waste.
  • Energy pyramid is never upside-down. Biomass and numbers pyramids can be inverted (one tree feeds thousands of insects).
  • Calculation: producers 10,000 kcal → primary 1,000 → secondary 100 → tertiary 10.
From: Energy and Ecosystems WS; study guide
Vocab: ecological pyramid, energy, biomass, pyramid of numbers
  1. Producers have 25,000 kcal. Energy for secondary consumers? → 25,000 × 0.1 × 0.1 = 250 kcal
  2. Why are food chains rarely longer than 4–5 levels? → Too little energy left at the top (90% lost each step)
  3. Which pyramid can never be inverted? → Energy pyramid

11. Biogeochemical (nutrient) cycles

  • Matter cycles, energy flows (energy enters as sunlight and leaves as heat).
  • Carbon: photosynthesis removes CO2; respiration, decomposition, burning release it. Reservoirs: atmosphere, oceans, fossil fuels, rocks.
  • Nitrogen: N2 gas can’t be used directly; nitrogen fixation by bacteria (legume roots) or lightning → ammonia/nitrates → plants → animals; denitrification returns N2. Often the limiting nutrient.
  • Water: evaporation, transpiration (plants), condensation, precipitation, runoff, groundwater.
  • Humans: fossil fuels ↑ CO2; fertilizer runoff → algae bloom → eutrophication (low O2); deforestation; positive: restoration, cover crops.
From: Biogeochemical Cycle Poster + Gallery Walk (10/27–30); CK-12 6.5–6.12 Reading Qs; Biomes Packet (due 11/3)
Vocab: biogeochemical cycle, carbon reservoir, nitrogen fixation, limiting nutrient, transpiration, eutrophication
  1. Which organisms make atmospheric N2 usable? → Nitrogen-fixing bacteria
  2. How does fertilizer runoff kill fish? → Algae bloom → decomposers use up O2 (eutrophication)
  3. Two processes that move carbon INTO the atmosphere? → Cellular respiration and burning fossil fuels (also decomposition)

Weak spot link · Membrane transport & osmosis (Unit 2) shows up again here

Where Unit 2 transport ideas reappear

  • Chemiosmosis = diffusion of H+ from high → low concentration through ATP synthase (a channel protein = facilitated diffusion).
  • H+ are pumped across the membrane against their gradient by the ETC = active transport (energy from electrons).
  • O2 and CO2 cross membranes and enter/leave leaves by simple diffusion through stomata.
  • Guard cells open stomata by taking in water (osmosis, turgor pressure); a wilting (plasmolyzed) plant closes stomata → less CO2 → less photosynthesis.
  • Tip: always say which way the substance moves (high→low = passive; low→high = active, needs energy).
From: Osmosis & Diffusion Lab, Cells & Transport Quiz (Unit 2)
Vocab: diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport, osmosis, concentration gradient, turgor
  1. Is H+ flowing through ATP synthase active or passive? → Passive (facilitated diffusion, down its gradient)
  2. A plant is watered with salt water. What happens to photosynthesis? → Water leaves cells by osmosis (hypertonic) → stomata close → less CO2 → photosynthesis drops
