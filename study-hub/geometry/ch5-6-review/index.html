Geometry Chapter 5–6 Test Review (Big Ideas Math Geometry)

Memory hook: Circumcenter = ⊥ bisectors = equal to Corners · Incenter = angle bisectors = Inside, equal to sides · Centroid = medians = 2 : 1 · Orthocenter = altitudes.

Chapter 5 · Congruent Triangles

5.1 Angles of Triangles

  • Triangle Sum: interior angles add to 180°.
  • Exterior Angle Thm: exterior angle = sum of the 2 remote interior angles.
  • Corollary: acute angles of a right triangle are complementary (sum 90°).
📐 Extend side AC past C to D: m∠BCD = m∠A + m∠B; m∠ACB + m∠BCD = 180°.
Watch out: adding the exterior angle to the adjacent interior angle and setting = 180 for the wrong angle; using 360.
  1. Exterior angle (5x + 4)°, remote angles (2x + 10)° and (x + 20)°. x? → 5x + 4 = 3x + 30 → x = 13 (exterior = 69°)
  2. Right triangle, one acute angle 37°. Other? → 53°

5.2 Congruent Polygons

  • Congruent ⇒ all corresponding sides and angles congruent. Order of letters matters: △ABC ≅ △DEF means A↔D, B↔E, C↔F.
  • Third Angles Thm: 2 pairs of angles ≅ ⇒ third pair ≅.
📐 Match vertices by the order of the congruence statement, not by the picture.
Watch out: matching by position in the drawing when the figure is rotated or flipped.
  1. △ABC ≅ △XYZ, AB = 3t − 2, XY = 2t + 5. t and AB? → t = 7, AB = 19
  2. m∠A = 40°, m∠B = 75° and △ABC ≅ △DEF. m∠F? → 65°

5.3 Proving Congruence by SAS

  • SAS: 2 sides and the included angle (between them) ≅ ⇒ triangles ≅.
  • Free facts: vertical angles ≅, reflexive (shared side ≅ itself).
📐 Angle mark must sit between the two marked sides.
Watch out: calling it SAS when the angle is not between the sides (that is SSA — not valid).
  1. AB ≅ DE, BC ≅ EF, ∠B ≅ ∠E. Congruent? → Yes, SAS (∠B is between AB and BC)
  2. Two segments bisect each other at M. Why are the two triangles ≅? → Halves ≅ (bisect) + vertical angles ≅ → SAS

5.4 Equilateral & Isosceles Triangles

  • Base Angles Thm: 2 sides ≅ ⇒ angles opposite them ≅ (and converse).
  • Equilateral ⇔ equiangular: every angle 60°.
📐 Legs AB = AC → ∠B = ∠C; vertex angle A = 180 − 2·base.
Watch out: putting the equal angles next to the wrong sides; forgetting the vertex angle is the odd one.
  1. Isosceles, vertex angle 44°. Base angles? → (180 − 44)/2 = 68°
  2. Equilateral triangle with sides 4x − 1 and x + 8. Side length? → x = 3, side 11

5.5 SSS & HL

  • SSS: 3 pairs of sides ≅.
  • HL (right triangles only): hypotenuse + one leg ≅.
  • Use Pythagorean a² + b² = c² to find missing sides.
📐 Right angle box + marked hypotenuse + marked leg → HL.
Watch out: using HL without a right angle; calling SSA valid (only HL is the special case).
  1. Right triangles, hypotenuses 13 ≅, one leg 5 each. Theorem? Other leg? → HL; leg = 12
  2. Sides 6, 8, 9 and 9, 6, 8. Congruent? → Yes, SSS

5.6 ASA & AAS

  • ASA: 2 angles + the included side.
  • AAS: 2 angles + a non-included side.
  • AAA and SSA do NOT prove congruence.
📐 Decision list: SSS · SAS · ASA · AAS · HL · or “not enough info”.
Watch out: mixing up ASA vs AAS (is the side between the two angles?); accepting AAA.
  1. ∠A ≅ ∠D, ∠B ≅ ∠E, BC ≅ EF. Theorem? → AAS (BC is not between ∠A and ∠B)
  2. All three angles ≅. Congruent? → Not enough information (could be different sizes)

5.7 Using Congruent Triangles (CPCTC)

  • Prove △≅ first, then CPCTC: corresponding parts of ≅ triangles are ≅.
  • Indirect measurement: build ≅ triangles to measure across a river/lake.
📐 Two-column proof: Given → shared side/vertical angles → SSS/SAS/ASA/AAS/HL → CPCTC.
Watch out: using CPCTC before proving the triangles congruent; skipping the “Given” or “Reflexive” reason.
  1. In a proof, ∠1 ≅ ∠2 because the triangles are ≅. Reason? → CPCTC
  2. Lake problem: △ABC ≅ △DEC by SAS, DE = 48 m. AB? → 48 m (CPCTC)

5.8 Coordinate Proofs

  • Distance d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²); midpoint ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2); slope for ⊥ (product −1).
  • Place figures smartly: vertex at origin, side on an axis.
📐 Compute all 3 side lengths for each triangle → SSS.
Watch out: subtracting coordinates in mixed order; forgetting to square negatives.
  1. Distance from (1, 2) to (4, 6)? → √(9 + 16) = 5
  2. Midpoint of (−2, 5) and (6, −1)? → (2, 2)

Chapter 6 · Relationships Within Triangles (6.1–6.3)

6.1 Perpendicular & Angle Bisectors

  • Perpendicular Bisector Thm: a point on the ⊥ bisector of AB is equidistant from A and B (PA = PB). Converse too.
  • Angle Bisector Thm: a point on an angle bisector is equidistant from the two sides of the angle. Converse too.
📐 Distance to a side = perpendicular segment (right-angle box), not a slanted segment.
Watch out: using a slanted segment as “distance”; setting PA = AM instead of PA = PB.
  1. P on ⊥ bisector of AB: PA = 3x + 2, PB = 5x − 10. PA? → x = 6, PA = 20
  2. D on bisector of ∠XYZ, distances to sides 2y + 7 and 4y − 1. Distance? → y = 4, distance 15

6.2 Bisectors of Triangles

  • Circumcenter = where the 3 ⊥ bisectors meet → equidistant from the 3 vertices (center of circle through vertices). Inside acute, on hypotenuse midpoint of right, outside obtuse.
  • Incenter = where the 3 angle bisectors meet → equidistant from the 3 sides (center of circle inside touching sides). Always inside.
📐 Incenter I, ID ⊥ side, JD along side: IJ² = ID² + JD².
Watch out: mixing up which point is equidistant from vertices vs sides; treating distance to a vertex as the inradius.
  1. P is the circumcenter, PA = 2x + 9, PB = 5x − 3. PC? → x = 4, PC = 17
  2. Incenter I, ID = 5 (to a side), JD = 12 along that side. IJ? → 13

6.3 Medians & Altitudes

  • Median: vertex → midpoint of opposite side. Medians meet at the centroid.
  • Centroid Thm: centroid is 2/3 of the way from each vertex: AP = 2·PD, AP = ⅔AD.
  • Centroid coordinates = average of vertices ((x₁+x₂+x₃)/3, (y₁+y₂+y₃)/3).
  • Altitude: vertex ⊥ to opposite side (line); altitudes meet at the orthocenter (inside acute, at right-angle vertex of right, outside obtuse).
📐 Median AD with centroid P: A—P—D, AP : PD = 2 : 1, AD = 3·PD.
Watch out: answering AD when asked AP (or the reverse); confusing median (midpoint) with altitude (perpendicular).
  1. Centroid P on median AD, PD = 7. AP and AD? → AP = 14, AD = 21
  2. Centroid of (0, 0), (6, 3), (3, 9)? → (3, 4)
